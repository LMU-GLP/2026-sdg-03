# Global Context and the UN Target

## The UN target

[SDG Target 3.4](../sources/un-sdg3-targets.md) commits UN member states to,
by 2030, "reduce by one third premature mortality from non-communicable
diseases through prevention and treatment and promote mental health and
well-being." Its only official indicator (3.4.2) is the **suicide mortality
rate** — there is no UN indicator here for depression or anxiety prevalence.
Adjacent targets cover substance abuse (3.5) and adolescent reproductive
health (3.7), but neither is a youth mental health measure. ([source](../sources/un-sdg3-targets.md))

## Global prevalence figures (WHO)

Per the [WHO adolescent mental health fact sheet](../sources/who-adolescent-mental-health.md)
(updated September 1, 2025):

- **One in seven 10–19-year-olds experiences a mental disorder**, accounting for **15% of the global burden of disease** in that age group.
- Depression: **1.3%** of 10–14-year-olds, **3.4%** of 15–19-year-olds.
- Anxiety disorders: **4.1%** of 10–14-year-olds, **5.3%** of 15–19-year-olds.
- **Suicide is the third leading cause of death among those aged 15–29 years old**, globally.

Direct quote: "one in seven 10-19-year-olds experiences a mental disorder,
accounting for 15% of the global burden of disease." ([source](../sources/who-adolescent-mental-health.md))

## Why these numbers can't be dropped straight into the LA or Korea pages

The WHO figures above are clinically diagnosed disorder prevalence, worldwide.
They are not the same measurement as the CDC's self-reported "persistent
sadness or hopelessness" survey used for the U.S. (see
[Los Angeles](los-angeles.md)), and not the same as Korea's per-100,000
suicide mortality rate or its separate self-reported depression survey (see
[South Korea](south-korea.md)). Per this wiki's citation rules, these figures
should be cited separately and never averaged or converted into one another.
