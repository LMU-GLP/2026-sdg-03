# Concept

## What it is

A school-based **VR/XR program** for Korean middle and high school students:
short, guided simulations of social scenarios that trigger isolation or
withdrawal (e.g. being excluded, rejected, or left out), letting a student
practice a response in a low-stakes, repeatable setting rather than facing
it cold for the first time in real life.

## Who it's for

**Students themselves**, directly — not parents, not teachers as the
primary user. This matches the [problem statement](problem-statement.md)'s
population exactly: middle and high school students, the same group in
which **25.7% of 54,170 surveyed students** reported feeling depression in
the past year. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))

## What it actually does

A student puts on a headset and works through a scripted social scenario
centered on isolation or withdrawal — the kind of moment (being excluded,
rejected, or ignored) that can compound the low mood the depression figure
above describes. The session is short and guided, not open-ended exposure
therapy for a diagnosed anxiety condition; the target is rehearsal and
practice, not treatment.

## How it works in the real world

- **Delivery:** schools own the VR headsets (e.g. Meta Quest–class devices)
  and run sessions during a counseling period or class, rather than relying
  on students' own phones or devices.
- **Safety net:** a counselor is present or waiting during every session,
  *and* the software itself pauses and surfaces a referral prompt
  (counselor contact or a warmline) if a student shows signs of distress.
  Both layers were chosen deliberately — the app doesn't rely on software
  alone to catch a bad reaction, and it doesn't rely on staffing alone
  either.
- **Why a human safety net matters here specifically:** this population's
  risk isn't just low mood — the same national data shows suicide has been
  the **leading cause of death for ages 9–24 for 14 consecutive years**, at
  **10.9 per 100,000** in 2024. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))
  An exercise that intentionally surfaces isolation and rejection scenarios
  for this population cannot ship without a real person reachable in the
  moment.

## What this deliberately leaves out

- **Not a diagnostic or clinical tool.** It doesn't assess or diagnose
  depression; it's a coping/practice exercise paired with a referral path,
  not a treatment or a replacement for clinical assessment.
- **Not a replacement for the counselor relationship.** The goal is to get
  more students into contact with a counselor sooner and with less friction
  — not to substitute the technology for ongoing human counseling.

## Open questions, not yet answered

- How Korea's exam-culture pressure (commonly discussed, but **not a figure
  in any source in this wiki**) relates to the isolation scenarios chosen
  here is unresolved — the team chose social isolation/rejection scenarios
  specifically because they connect to the depression evidence we have, not
  because we have sourced evidence about exam stress.
- **No Korea-based program precedent exists yet in `sources/`.** The only
  existing-program source in this wiki, the Youstar Foundation warmline, is
  LA-based. ([source](../sources/youstar-foundation-warmline.md)) This
  concept has no cited prior-art example of a Korea-based school mental
  health program to point to.
- Cost and school procurement of VR headsets at scale is a real constraint
  this concept does not yet address, and no source in this wiki speaks to
  headset cost or Korean school technology budgets.
