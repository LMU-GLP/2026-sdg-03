# Los Angeles vs. South Korea: Comparing the Evidence

**Short version: we cannot line these figures up in one table and call it a
comparison.** Each region's source measures something different, at a
different level (national vs. county), with a different method. Per this
wiki's citation rules, figures from different measurement systems are kept
separate below rather than compared directly or averaged.

## What each source actually measures

| Region | Source | What it measures | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States (not LA-specific) | [CDC 2023 YRBS](../sources/la-youth-mental-health.md) | Self-reported persistent sadness/hopelessness, suicide ideation, via survey | National |
| South Korea | [Korea Times / Ministry of Gender Equality and Family](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md) | Suicide mortality rate per 100,000; separate self-reported depression survey | National |
| LA County (Korean American community specifically) | [Youstar Foundation source](../sources/youstar-foundation-warmline.md) | Suicide rate ranking among Asian ethnic groups | County, one ethnic community |
| Global | [WHO fact sheet](../sources/who-adolescent-mental-health.md) | Diagnosed mental disorder prevalence | Worldwide |

## What each region's evidence says, on its own terms

**South Korea:** Suicide has been the **leading cause of death** for ages
9–24 for 14 straight years, at **10.9 per 100,000** — more than three times
the rate of the next-leading cause (safety accidents, 3.3 per 100,000).
Separately, **25.7%** of surveyed middle/high schoolers reported depression
in the past year. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))

**United States (national, not LA-specific):** **40%** of high schoolers
report persistent sadness or hopelessness (down from 42% in 2021); it's
**53%** for female students specifically (down from 57%). Suicide
consideration among female students dropped from 30% to 27%. Bullying and
feeling unsafe at school both got *worse* over the same period.
([source](../sources/la-youth-mental-health.md))

**Los Angeles specifically:** We have no youth mental health prevalence
figure at the LA or LA County level. The one LA-specific figure we have is
narrower and different in kind: Korean Americans in LA County have "the
highest suicide rate among Asian groups" in the county.
([source](../sources/youstar-foundation-warmline.md))

## What we can and can't conclude

- We **cannot** say which region's youth are "worse off" — the U.S. figure
  is a self-reported symptom survey, Korea's headline figure is a mortality
  rate, and neither is available at the LA-specific level for a fair fight.
- We **can** say South Korea's data centers mortality (suicide as the
  literal leading cause of death for the age group), while the U.S. data
  centers self-reported emotional state. That's a real difference in what
  gets tracked and reported, not just a difference in numbers.
- We **can** say LA's most specific, sourced data point is about one
  immigrant community's suicide risk, not youth broadly — which is itself a
  gap worth naming if the team picks LA as its region.
