# South Korea

## Key figures

From [the Korea Times report](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md) on
Ministry of Gender Equality and Family youth statistics:

- **Suicide has been the leading cause of death among Korean youth for 14 consecutive years, since 2011.**
- Deaths among those aged 9–24 in 2024: **1,749 total.**
- Leading causes of death, per 100,000: **suicide 10.9**, safety accidents 3.3, cancer 2.6 — suicide claimed more than three times as many young lives as safety accidents.
- A Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency survey of **54,170 middle and high school students** found **25.7%** — more than one in four — reported experiencing feelings of depression in the past year.
- The 9–24 population is 7.4 million, 14.4% of Korea's total, down 46.5% from four decades ago.

Direct quote: "Suicide has been the leading cause of death among Korean
youth for 14 consecutive years since 2011, claiming more than three times as
many young lives as safety accidents have in 2024." ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))

## Context from the same release

The same Ministry release found Korean students unusually willing to
engage publicly on social issues: 80.4% said youth should make their voices
heard on social and political issues (girls 83.9%, boys 77.1%), and 97.3%
said men and women should have equal rights in all areas. Multicultural
students rose 145% in a decade, from 82,536 (2015) to 202,208 (2025), now 4%
of all students. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))

## A figure to be careful with

Some outside summaries claim suicide accounted for "60.2% of all deaths" in
this age group. That figure does not appear in our source article and could
not be verified — do not use it. Use the per-100,000 rates above instead,
which are stated directly. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))
