# Problem Statement

## Region

**South Korea.**

## Who specifically

**Middle and high school students in South Korea.** This is the population
our clearest rate-based evidence covers, distinct from the broader 9–24
age band that our mortality figures describe.

## How many

**54,170 middle and high school students** were surveyed by the Korea
Disease Control and Prevention Agency, and **25.7%** of them — more than
one in four — reported experiencing feelings of depression in the past
year. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md)) We do not have a
national total enrollment figure for Korean middle/high schoolers in our
sources, so this statement uses the surveyed count and rate directly rather
than projecting a national total.

## What our sources say

Suicide has been the **leading cause of death among Korean youth for 14
consecutive years, since 2011**. In 2024, among the broader 9–24 age
population (7.4 million, 14.4% of Korea's total), the death rate from
suicide was **10.9 per 100,000** — more than three times the rate from
safety accidents (3.3 per 100,000), the next-leading cause.
([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))

Within that same national picture, the population this statement commits
to — middle and high school students — shows the problem while still
alive: **25.7% of 54,170 surveyed students** reported feelings of
depression in the past year. ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))
Read together, these two figures describe the same national trend at two
points on one path: a country where a quarter of surveyed students already
report depression, and where, over 14 years, that unaddressed distress has
tracked through to suicide becoming the leading cause of death for people
still years away from adulthood.

## Scope notes

- **These two figures come from the same source and the same national
  survey effort, but they are not the same measurement** — one is a
  mortality rate per 100,000 for ages 9–24, the other is a self-reported
  survey rate for surveyed middle/high schoolers specifically. They are
  presented together for context, not combined into a single number, per
  this wiki's citation rules.
- **A figure to avoid:** some outside summaries claim suicide was "60.2% of
  all deaths" in this age group. That number does not appear in our source
  and could not be verified — don't use it.
  ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md))
- **We do not have a Korea-based program or service source** in this wiki
  yet. Our one "existing program" source, the Youstar Foundation warmline,
  operates in Los Angeles for Korean American immigrants — relevant context,
  but not a South Korea–based example. ([source](../sources/youstar-foundation-warmline.md))
  If the team's solution needs a Korea-based precedent to point to, that's
  an open research gap, not something this statement can currently cite.
- **The UN's own target here (SDG 3.4)** is measured only by suicide
  mortality rate — it has no indicator for depression prevalence, which is
  half of the evidence this statement rests on.
  ([source](../sources/un-sdg3-targets.md))

## The statement

> In South Korea, suicide has been the leading cause of death for people
> aged 9–24 for 14 consecutive years running, at a rate of 10.9 per 100,000
> in 2024 — more than three times the rate from safety accidents. Within
> that same national picture, a 2024 survey of 54,170 middle and high
> school students found that 25.7%, more than one in four, reported feeling
> depression in the past year. These are Korean students describing
> significant emotional distress years before the outcomes tracked by the
> mortality data, in a country whose national health surveys already
> measure both — but which has no dedicated Korea-based mental health
> program in our evidence yet, and whose UN-aligned target (SDG 3.4) tracks
> only the mortality side of this problem, not the depression side.
> ([source](../sources/korea-youth-suicide.md), [source](../sources/un-sdg3-targets.md))
