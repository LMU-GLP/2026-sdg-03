# SDG 3: Good Health and Well-Being — Targets and Indicators

- **Source:** United Nations, Department of Economic and Social Affairs, Sustainable Development Goals
- **URL:** https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal3
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures — targets relevant to youth mental health

- **Target 3.4:** "By 2030, reduce by one third premature mortality from non-communicable diseases through prevention and treatment and promote mental health and well-being."
  - Indicator 3.4.2: Suicide mortality rate.
- **Target 3.5:** "Strengthen the prevention and treatment of substance abuse, including narcotic drug abuse and harmful use of alcohol."
  - Indicator 3.5.1: Coverage of treatment interventions for substance use disorders.
  - Indicator 3.5.2: "Alcohol per capita consumption (aged 15 years and older) within a calendar year in litres of pure alcohol."
- **Target 3.7:** "By 2030, ensure universal access to sexual and reproductive health-care services, including for family planning, information and education, and the integration of reproductive health into national strategies and programmes."
  - Indicator 3.7.2: "Adolescent birth rate (aged 10-14 years; aged 15-19 years) per 1,000 women in that age group."

## Direct quote

> "By 2030, reduce by one third premature mortality from non-communicable diseases through prevention and treatment and promote mental health and well-being."

## Note on scope

SDG 3 has no target written specifically as "youth mental health." Target 3.4
is the one that names mental health directly, and its own indicator (3.4.2)
measures it only through suicide mortality — there is no UN indicator here
for depression, anxiety, or non-fatal mental health burden. Any team claim
about "the UN target for youth mental health" should point to 3.4 and be
explicit that the official indicator is a suicide rate, not a mental-health
prevalence measure.

## Summary

SDG Target 3.4 is the UN's official commitment to promoting mental health and
well-being, but its only indicator is the suicide mortality rate — there's no
global target for depression or anxiety prevalence specifically. Targets 3.5
(substance abuse) and 3.7 (adolescent reproductive health) are adjacent but
address different problems, not youth mental health directly.
