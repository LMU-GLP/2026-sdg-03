# 2023 Youth Risk Behavior Survey Results

- **Source:** U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), published September 29, 2024
- **URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/results/2023-yrbs-results.html
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures (2021 → 2023)

- Students overall with **persistent feelings of sadness or hopelessness: 42% → 40%**
- **Female students** who felt persistently sad or hopeless: **57% → 53%**
- Female students who **seriously considered attempting suicide: 30% → 27%**
- Hispanic students: persistently sad or hopeless 46% → 42%; seriously considered suicide 22% → 18%
- Black students who attempted suicide: 14% → 10%
- Getting worse over the same period: bullied at school **15% → 19%**; missed school due to safety concerns **9% → 13%**; threatened or injured with a weapon at school 7% → 9%
- **More than three-quarters of students reported frequent social media use**, which was associated with higher prevalence of being bullied, feelings of sadness and hopelessness, and suicide risk.

## ⚠️ This is national data, not Los Angeles data

The YRBS is a nationally representative survey of U.S. high school students. It
does not break out Los Angeles County. **Finding an L.A.-specific figure is this
team's first job on Friday.** When you read your wiki, if there's no local
number in it, ask Claude for one directly. Good places to look: the L.A. County
Department of Public Health, LAUSD's own student surveys, or the California
Healthy Kids Survey. Be careful with numbers you find on therapy
clinic or treatment center websites — several circulating L.A. mental health
statistics trace back to marketing pages with no primary source, and a judge can
check.

## Summary

Roughly two in five American high schoolers report persistent sadness or
hopelessness, and the figure is twice as high for girls as for boys. The
national picture improved slightly from 2021 to 2023 on mental health, but got
worse on bullying and on students staying home because school felt unsafe.
