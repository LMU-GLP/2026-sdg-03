# Youstar Foundation's Warmline — Korean-Language Mental Health Support in Koreatown, LA

- **Source:** LA Local, "Korean American Warmline Challenges Mental Health Stigma in Koreatown"
- **URL:** https://thelalocal.org/neighborhoods/koreatown/korean-language-barrier-mental-health-warmline-stigma-youstar-foundation/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- The warmline is staffed by **nearly 70 volunteer counselors**, most of whom speak Korean.
- Hours: **8 a.m. to 9 p.m.**, with a stated plan to expand to 24-hour availability within three years.
- **Korean Americans in Los Angeles County experience the highest suicide rate among Asian groups.**
- The service is free and operates in both Korean and English.

## What it is

Founded by Soh Yun Park and her husband Sang Kyun Park, the Youstar
Foundation runs a "warmline" — a support line pitched a tier below a crisis
hotline — aimed specifically at Korean-speaking and Korean American
immigrants in LA County. It grew out of a community-service magazine the
founders ran to connect people with local resources.

## Direct quote

> "They hide it, which prevents them from getting help." — Soh Yun Park, on why Korean Americans avoid seeking mental health support

## Note on scope

This is a program operating in Los Angeles, serving a Korean-speaking
immigrant population — it sits at the intersection of the two regions this
team is comparing. The "highest suicide rate among Asian groups" claim is
county-wide and about Korean Americans in LA specifically; it is not a
South Korea national figure and should not be conflated with the rates in
[the Korea Times source](korea-youth-suicide.md).

## Summary

The Youstar Foundation runs a free, Korean-language warmline in LA County,
staffed by nearly 70 volunteers, built specifically because Korean American
immigrants in LA have the highest suicide rate of any Asian group in the
county and cultural stigma keeps them from seeking help. It's a working
example of a low-cost, volunteer-driven service model rather than a clinical
one.
